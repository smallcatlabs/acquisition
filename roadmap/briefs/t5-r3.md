## Goal

When this round is done, the scripted-server scenario families are
rows of the wire table through the door: a script, the requests a
frontend sends, and what crosses the boundary back (each job's end
and retries, a marker in its payload, the words its error must and
must not carry, the journal's sends with their waits and shapes, the
daemon's halt and degraded endpoints through its status and
dashboard), each row run on the parking clock under the park hook in
milliseconds. The three wire twins stage at the boundary what
`responses.rs` judges as a function, which stays the classification's
component table. The tests that read the daemon's internals (a mark
on disk, the log, the choke's state, a private call standing in for
a login) stay component tests saying so, their fixed sleeps awaited
states. No production behaviour changes; no word a user meets changes.

## Do

1. **The row, widened.** A row is a sequence of steps, each a kind
   the worker names in one enum: a batch of requests sent before any
   is awaited (priority needs two jobs queued together, 245–252; FIFO
   needs them competing, 620–625); the jobs' expected states awaited
   (terminal through `await_terminal`, Waiting through `await_job`:
   a block's sibling is left Waiting, 770); a `Request::Cancel`, a
   `Request::ResetTripwire`, a `Request::DaemonStatus` read, a
   `Request::Dashboard` read; and a second lifetime: a second door
   opened on the same `state_path` with `Server::None`, its
   `DaemonStatus.rails.halted` read and the door finished, while the
   first door is still live (the 5xx case reads its clean second
   lifetime before the reset, since the reset clears the persisted
   marks, `rails.rs:698`; the block case reads its halted one). What
   a row asserts, each optional: per job in id order (the submitted
   and the probes the daemon makes, as `Request::List` lists them)
   its end state, its retries, a marker its payload must carry, the
   words its error must carry and must not; the journal's projection,
   `[method, path, status, ok, counted, wait_ms, shape]` (`ok` for the
   truncated 200, `shape` `None` where the journal has none);
   `forbids`, fragments that must appear nowhere in the whole journal
   text, in `Dashboard.errors` nor in a lifetime's halt words (the 3xx
   sentinel, 1155–1165; the block's body and header fragments,
   821–830); `virtual_at_most`, a bound on the parking clock's advance
   over the row (the exhaustion case's "no final sleep", 533, never a
   wall-clock read); `halted`, words `DaemonStatus.rails.halted` must
   carry or `None`; `degraded`, the endpoints `Dashboard.degraded_
   endpoints` must name. The runner subscribes to the daemon's events
   once, before any submit, keeps that receiver for the row, counts
   terminal events by job id and rejects a lag; it expects exactly one
   terminal event for every job the row ends and none for a job it
   leaves Waiting (`await_terminal`'s own receiver is a hint and is
   dropped on return, `door.rs:570`, so it records nothing). Priority
   and account come from `Request::Submit` as a frontend sends them.
   The runner reads nothing through `door.daemon()`.

2. **The rows.** Every family test whose assertions the boundary
   reaches becomes rows, one per case, in `wire_rows`, each named for
   what it pins and citing the ruling or ground truth the test cited:
   the 429 retry bound and its exhaustion (475, 510), the FIFO
   identity (609), the four unusable `Retry-After`s (552), the HEAD
   429 under hold (1173) and the two cooldown cases (1209, through
   `degraded`), the three block cases (701: the halt through
   `halted`, the error's cause and remedy words, the sibling left
   Waiting; the mark surviving a restart as the second lifetime's
   `halted` carrying the cause; `forbids` the body and header
   fragments; and, for the 1015, the GET's hold of at least 30 s,
   783, as a boundary fact: after `ResetTripwire` a further fetch
   whose journal `wait_ms` is the 429's hold as C19 prices a
   `Retry-After` of 30, written from the ruling), the four
   Cloudflare 5xx cases (941: the halt, the sibling Waiting, the
   second lifetime reporting no halt before the reset, then
   `ResetTripwire` and the sibling landing, the script's pending GET
   answered then), the origin's three (268, 637, 1066), GGG's four
   4xx (854), the priority order (222), the 3xx (1119: the error's
   words, `forbids` the sentinel over the journal and `Dashboard.
   errors`), the HEAD 403 (1489). A row's expected journal and words
   are written from the rulings and the old test's assertions, never
   from running the door (an oracle, `GLOSSARY.md`). The block rows'

## Taken from the review

The five findings of `roadmap/reviews/t5-r3-brief-66086b68.md`:

1. One terminal event per job contradicted rows that leave a job
   Waiting, and the waiter records nothing: one subscription before
   any submit, counted by id, one for an ended job and none for a
   Waiting one (Do 1, done-when).
2. The sequence model lacked batches and checkpoints: a row is a
   sequence of steps, with a second lifetime read before the reset
   (Do 1, Do 2).
3. 701's GET hold after a 1015 lost its holder: a post-reset fetch
   whose journal wait is the 429's hold (Do 2).
4. The projection lacked `ok` and the forbidden fragments: `ok`
   added, `forbids` over the journal, the dashboard's errors and a
   lifetime's halt (Do 1, Do 2).
5. `forget_degraded()` is at 1278 (Read).
