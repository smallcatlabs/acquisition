# 1 — The governance reset (a Fable design session)

## Goal
The framework keeps what demonstrably caught something and retires what
was only ever satisfied (P6). After it, a session opens one auto-loaded
file of about 14 KB and an area file without history, finds the backlog
in GitHub issues, and writes back to code, a test, a generated
reference and at most one entry or one issue. The owner accepted this
shape on 2026-10-19 ("I accept all your recommendations exactly as
proposed").

## Read
- `roadmap/STATE-2026-10-19.md`, report 3 whole (the proposal is its
  section 7), and the synthesis points 2, 3 and 9.
- `roadmap/parks-triage.md`: every park's status and fate.
- `AGENTS.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, every `decisions/*.md`, the eight slice
  records' shapes-of-fault tables, `tools/docs-check.sh`,
  `tools/bothy.py`, the six skills, `TESTING-NOTES.md`,
  `LIVE-TESTING.md`.
- Memory: the owner's guiding light (simplicity over mechanism: a
  change must remove agent work and reuse a shape the tool has).

## Input
Phase 0 reviewed and merged, so the stale lines are already true.

## Do
1. Draft the new `AGENTS.md` (≤ 14 KB, auto-loaded): orientation, the
   five invariants, the hard rules (no real daemon from an agent shell;
   build order; the gate), the crate table, the cross-cutting rulings as
   one-liners with a *Why*, where things live, the roadmap pointer. The
   principles become three lines. Registry grammar and park rules move
   into `bothy.py`'s docstring.
2. Write the entry rule and apply it to a sample of ten: a ruling in
   plain declaratives, a *Why*, a *Pinned*; no dates, amendment trails,
   owner quotes, seat call numbers or measurements (git and the commit
   messages hold them); a gloss of two to five words on every cited id.
   Put the sample to the owner with the draft.
3. The parks: from the triage table, the "not doing" list per area (the
   15 to 20 real boundaries, one line each, no evidence prose), the
   deletion list (the rot rows and the withdrawn one, the latter as a
   refused ruling), and the issue list for the rest with labels
   `area:*` and `trigger:{user,seat,consumer,gui,live}`. The owner reads
   the deletion list before anything is deleted.
4. The records: `REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` (every shapes-of-fault table,
   grouped by area, each row with its holding test; about 12 KB) and
   `HISTORY.md` (one line per slice: commit range, close date). The
   eight records are then deleted, cited by commit where a ruling cites
   them.
5. The notes: apply the existing rule; a note whose rulings are
   harvested is deleted and cited as "note NN at `<commit>`". Note 36
   keeps only its launch-day step or moves to an issue.
6. The checks: budgets retired except on `AGENTS.md`; the entry limit
   kept with history forbidden, or raised to 1,000 bytes; the index
   generated by `bothy.py` instead of checked; the dependency-edge check
   and the trade self-tests moved out of the docs check into their own
   scripts; a cheap scan of the skills against test-enforced rules.
7. The skills: `session-close` reduced to gate, commit, ledger row;
   `LIVE-TESTING.md` folded into the live-run skill; the run ledger
   stays append-only.
8. `TESTING-NOTES.md` cut to its purpose and "looks like coverage,
   isn't".
9. Hand the migration to Opus workers as steps 1b to 1e, each a brief
   from this template, run one after another in the main tree (they all
   touch the same files).

## Done when
- The owner has ruled on the `AGENTS.md` draft, the entry sample, the
  deletion list and the issue list.
- The migration briefs exist and the first has started.
- The roadmap's phase 1 row names the migration steps and their order.

## Not in scope
- Rewriting the code's doc comments (the "Decisions as recorded" copies
  are trimmed as files are touched in phase 2, under the entry rule).
- Any code change.

## Rules
`TEMPLATE.md`, and: the design is put to the owner before a file is
deleted; a deletion names the commit that holds the deleted text.

## Report
The drafts and lists above, in the repository as the owner reads them.
