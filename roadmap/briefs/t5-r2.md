# t5-r2 — `Life` on the clock (#141, round 2)

The second round of t5's design (`t5-design.md` §2 item 5, §4 round 2;
ruled 2026-10-31, C158). Written from one read-only inventory of the
lifecycle's callers at `37275136` and the tree at `508022ee`, every
line printed; round 1 closed (`LEDGER.md`, `t5-r1.md`).

## Goal

When this round is done, the daemon's lifecycle driver sleeps through
the daemon's `Clock`: its three timers (the idle deadline, the drain's
deadline, the quiet wait's look) and the exit's deadline are instants
on that clock, so on the parking clock they park and on the system
clock they run as today; the exit bound's OS thread stays real
(C133). Every caller of the driver moves with it, since on the manual
clock a driver on the clock fires at once (the spike, `4-0-spike.md`
"Answer"): the drain scenarios of `exit.rs` run through the door in
virtual time, the driver's component tests on a parking clock they
drive by hand, the multi-thread ones likewise. The `settle` sleeps
of the moved tests retire, held by what the journal and the log
order say; the one elapsed lower bound on the tokio drain becomes a
bound on the clock. No production behaviour changes but the clock a
timer reads; no word a user meets changes; no log line a test reads
changes.

## Do

1. **The driver on the clock.** `ChokePoint::sleep_until(Instant)`
   beside its `sleep` (choke.rs:316–324). In `server.rs`: `Driver`'s
   `idle`, `drain` and `looking` become `Option<std::time::Instant>`;
   `Arm(Idle)` reads `choke.now()` (851–857); `Arm(Drain)` reads
   `exit.deadline`, now a std instant (861–866); `look` arms the next
   look from `choke.now()` (927); `until` sleeps through
   `choke.sleep_until` (1010–1015). `life`'s doc (674–676) and
   `Exit`'s (209–211) say what is now true: the driver sleeps through
   the daemon's clock, which parks in a scenario and runs in
   production; the manual test clock cannot drive it.
2. **The exit's deadline on the clock.** `Exit::deadline` becomes
   `OnceLock<std::time::Instant>`; `Exit::arm(why, now: Instant)`
   sets it from the `now` its caller passes (`daemon.choke.now()` at
   every ingress: `begin_exit` 546, the watcher 218 on its own thread,
   the idle commit's `begin_exit`); the bound thread's `at` stays on
   `std::time::Instant::now()` and `std::thread::sleep` (270–281,
   C133). `EXIT_BOUND`'s doc (190) says the bound is on the daemon's
   clock, its thread on the wall's. `a_second_begin_exit_returns_at_
   once` (367) compares the std instant.
3. **The door's lifecycle.** `DoorBuilder::life(knob: Duration)`
   (off by default: a scenario that does not ask gets no idle timer,
   so the hook never advances it an hour) and `DoorBuilder::exit_
   bound(Duration)` (through a new builder option, `Daemon::builder`'s
   `exit`, replacing the tests' `Arc::get_mut` patch of `exit` where
   the door builds; the patch stays for the component tests); `run`
   spawns `life` with the recording leave when asked. `door.stop()`:
   what the bootstrap stop does (`request_stop`, 536–540; the stop
   is the bootstrap plane's, which `Request` does not carry), the
   frontend's `acq daemon stop`. `door.await_leave() -> Option<
   InFlight>`: the recorded leave's cut, awaited under `HANG`.
   `finish` after a leave: the dispatcher already gone, the oracles
   on the journal as always.
4. **The drain scenarios through the door**, each a plain `#[test]`
   on `ParkingClock` under the hook, with no `settle` and no sleep:
   - the held drains (91, 122, 508, 571): the work on the wire or in
     a landing holds the clock (the hook's `held_by_send` or
     `held_by_blocking`), the drain parks its look; the scenario
     releases the work; `await_leave` is `None` (clean), and the log
     order holds that the drain waited: the job's or the login's
     landing line before "exit: nothing in flight". The `Own` fixture
     for the two with their own listener (508, 571), a `std` listener
     bound before `run` as the door's doc says; `Script` for the two
     scripted.
   - the turn-aways (229, 259, 457): through the door; 457 leaves the
     system clock (the pacing slice parks on the parking clock and
     the hook advances to it after the stop, so the turn-away is seen
     in virtual time); its 5 ms poll (467–479) becomes `await_terminal`
     or a state read through `request`; the 50 ms sleep of
     `park_a_job_at_the_token_route` (199) becomes an awaited state.
   - the cut (289): the stuck job's `sleep` kind sleeps real time
     beside the clock (kinds.rs:571–577), which the hook does not
     count, so virtual time reaches the bound and the drain cuts;
     `waited >= bound` (309) becomes `clock.now() - began >= bound`
     on the parking clock, a bound on the clock, not the wall.
   - the refusal (323) and the second begin (367): through the door's
     `stop()`, their assertions as they are.
   `BOUND` and `HELD` keep their names and values as the scenarios'
   exit bounds, now virtual: a scenario that must not cut uses `HELD`,
   one that must uses its own short bound.
5. **The component tests on the parking clock.** The driver's
   `LifeProbe` tests (909, 933, 968–1004, 1013, 1061) stay
   `#[tokio::test]` component tests (their boundary is the driver's
   probe, which the door does not expose; their module doc says so)
   but on `ParkingClock` through `exit_daemon_on`: a knob or a bound
   at zero is a deadline already due, which `ParkedSleep` answers at
   once (harness.rs:216–223's registration: ready when `deadline <=
   now`), so none of them advances the clock; `exit_daemon`'s
   default clock becomes `ParkingClock`. The two multi-thread tests
   of `exit.rs` (388, 1099) likewise on `ParkingClock`: 388's drain
   never reaches its deadline on a parked clock, which is the case
   it stages (the bound's thread ends the process); 1099's judge is
   held on `shared` and its signal is handed by hand.
6. **`credentials.rs`'s drains on the parking clock**, off the door
   (four workers): `restoring_daemon` (564) and the `c148` stop test
   (318) on `ParkingClock`; where a drain must complete (357, 781),
   the test drives the clock by hand after its release, `advance_to_
   next` under `HANG` until the drain resolves (the drain's look, a
   parked 10 ms, is the only sleeper beside the idle timer, which
   `advance_to_next` reaches second); 357's 100 ms sleep (358) becomes
   a state the test awaits (the drain polled pending once, as 781
   does, or the held clear's arrival).
7. **Retirements**, each with its C154 line in the commit: `settle`
   (82–83) and `spawn_drain` (77) retired; held by the log-order
   assertions and `await_leave` of the moved scenarios; `wait_state`'s
   uses (65–72) that the door replaces, held by `await_terminal`; the
   `SystemClock` import of `exit.rs` (464) gone. `ManualClock` is not
   retired this round (round 5): its remaining users are not `Life`
   callers.
8. `tools/gate.sh` once per commit (two at least: the driver and the
   exit on the clock with the component tests moved, the tree green
   with the scenarios still on the harness where they must wait;
   then the door's lifecycle and the scenarios through it with the
   retirements); the test stage's seconds and `exit.rs`'s wall time
   before and after in the report.
