## Goal

When this round is done, every login and session scenario whose
property crosses the frontend boundary runs through the door: a login
is `Request::AuthStart`, the browser's callback played by the scenario,
and `await_auth` over `AuthStatus`; a refresh is `Request::AuthCheck`;
a logout is `Request::AuthLogout`; what a scenario reads is the
protocol's answers, the journal, the fixture's record of the wire (the
account a bearer or a grant named, in order) and the scenario's own
credential store and the index on disk (the store's read API, C12).
The cancel family on the account server runs through the door (two
of its scenarios and the account server's two C144 tests with it).
One door primitive, the runtime quiet under a held send, answers what
three private polls and three fixed sleeps stood for ("the waiter
joined", "the parked refresh reached the gate"). The tests that need
the daemon's internals (a fabricated abort of a refresh owner, a
completion superseded by generation, a freeze between a profile's
write and its landing, a store hook, a function, the callback
listener's own reads) say which boundary in a doc line and stay
component tests. No production behaviour changes; the one production
edit is the builder's `store_dir` option; no word a user meets
changes.

## Do

1. **The door's additions**, each with its rule in its doc:
   - `Door::quiet`: resolves at the next park at which nothing can
     run, whatever holds the clock (a send, a blocking task, a hold),
     and that park moves nothing. The hook checks it before the holds.
     Its rule: a scenario calls it only after the arrival barrier of
     a request it holds at its own fixture (`Fixture::Account` or
     `Delayed`'s `arrived`) has fired; before that a park may be a
     socket read's, and `quiet` would resolve early. `settle` stays
     what it is (the quiet park at which nothing is in flight); each
     doc names the other. Under a held send every task the daemon
     runs for a request has either answered or parked on what it
     waits for (the flight's receiver, the gate's queue), so the park
     is the exact signal that the waiters joined and the parked job
     reached the gate: the runtime's own word, as C158 states it,
     never a count of receivers.
   - `DoorBuilder::store_dir(path)`, over a production
     `Builder::store_dir` in `daemon.rs` beside `world` (which keeps
     setting it): the field exists; no behaviour changes. The four
     `Arc::get_mut` patches in `login.rs` (58, 696, 761, 1008) and
     `sessions.rs` (871) go.
