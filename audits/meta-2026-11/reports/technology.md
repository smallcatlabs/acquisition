# Technology slice: verdicts

| | Verdict | Deciding number |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **H1** Rust for the core is not worth questioning | **HOLDS** | Of 5,723 commits, the record of fights with ownership is still the retrospective's 9 borrow mentions in 3,212. The cost is in the build: a gate `test` stage at median 39 s, and 36.6 h of agent wall time in `cargo nextest` over six weeks. |
| **H2** the pages should not be hand-built HTML strings | **NARROWED** | 795,121 of the 1,049,903 golden bytes (76%) are whole HTML pages. A change to `web/` touches a median of 10 files. The strings are not what costs; the whole page as the unit of both navigation and test is. |
| **H3** the Python is a second codebase outside the gate | **NARROWED** | About 2.46 MB of TOML that the binary embeds is generated by Python. The 918 KB of it from `class-table.py`, `defence-table.py` and `trade-forms-table.py` is checked against its generator only on a machine that holds the `poe1` clone. CI has no such clone, and its log prints `ok` for the skip. |

## H1: HOLDS

Rust holds things here by construction that the project would otherwise hold by grep or by review:
- Check 5 in `tools/arch-check.sh` (the dependency edges, read from `cargo metadata`, with the compiler refusing an undeclared crate) and check 15 (one production transport).
- `Secret` has no `Serialize`.
- The closed `Fault` is matched exhaustively on three frontends.
- `deny(clippy::unwrap_used)` stands in five crates: plan, protocol, search, store and verbs (`git grep -ln "deny(clippy::unwrap_used"`).
- Checked arithmetic turned hostile headers into test panics (retrospective §3).

The cost is Cargo and compile time, not the language at the keyboard:
- Gate stage medians over 229 full green runs (awk over `runs/gate/verdicts.tsv`): test 39 s (p90 62), build 4 s, doc 13 s.
- `target/` measures 16 GB today (`du -sh target`).

The only honest case for another language is iteration latency: the retrospective's TypeScript study puts the inner loop at 5 to 15 s against Rust's 61 to 140 s. That buys speed, not safety, and that study shows the limiter opening on a `NaN`. Nothing I measured overturns `roadmap/LANGUAGE-RETROSPECTIVE-2026-11-09.md:665-694`.

## H2: NARROWED

**What is there, counted by me:**
- `crates/acquisition-cli/src/web/` is 12,889 lines.
  - Rust: `page.rs` 5,630, `mod.rs` 4,550, `form.rs` 1,203, `fill.rs` 581, `from.rs` 346 (`wc -l`).
  - Assets: four scripts of 397 lines, CSS of 165 and a 17-line HTML template.
- `page.rs` has 309 lines with a tag, 452 `format!` and 301 `push_str` (`grep -c`).
- Of the code lines, 763 of `page.rs` and 901 of `mod.rs` are doc comments.
- Tests: `tests/web.rs` 8,339 lines with 52 `#[test]`; `tests/browser.rs` 2,366 lines with 16.

**Layout against semantics.** `page.rs` is presentation by its own doc ("A page sorts, filters, groups and composes nothing", `page.rs:19`). `form.rs` and `fill.rs` (about 1,800 lines) are the form's fold and its inverse, the only part of `web/` that is semantics. `mod.rs` is routes and handlers.

**Escaping is held by convention.**
- There is one `esc()` (`page.rs:52`), called 298 times. A `String` is a `String`: no type, no lint.
- The test side has its own copy of the escaper (`escaped`, `tests/web.rs:8129`).
- The structural hold is the harness's scan: every `<script ` tag on a recorded page must name a listed source (`tests/harness/mod.rs:1482-1488`). It only sees what the recorded pages contain.
- `mock-items.json` contains no `<` (`grep -c '<'` gives 0), and no test feeds a hostile item name, tab name or note through a page.
- I spot-read about 20 non-`esc` `format!` sites and found no hole. I did not audit all of them.
- This matters because C138 says this page can cause GGG sends through apply.

**How a change spreads** (`git log -40 -- crates/acquisition-cli/src/web/`):
- 520 file-touches, a median of 10 per commit.
- 197 golden touches, about 5 per commit.
- 36 of the 40 commits also touched a test file.
- 113 of the 738 commits touching `crates/` since 2026-10-25 touched `web/`.

**The options:**
- **(a) Stay as is.** Costs nothing new, and keeps the 303-and-whole-page-per-act model that the trial shows is the slow part. The server answers in 30 to 160 ms; the browser's swap takes 3 to 4 ms (`trials/gui-in-place.md:15-26`).
- **(b) A typed markup crate (maud or askama).**
  - Buys escaping by type, which retires the convention above.
  - Deletes no golden: the same pages come out, regenerated.
  - Adds a proc-macro to the crate that is already in 46% of commits, so compile time goes up. Not measured.
  - Does nothing for the interactive feel.
  - No ruling changes. Tried by rewriting `card()` (`page.rs:2381`); undone by reverting.
  - My judgment: it fixes a risk, not the problem.
- **(c) Fragments swapped in place, Rust owning everything.**
  - Gives the C++-like feel the trial measured.
  - Adds one script.
  - Deletes landing machinery: `toggle_item`, `at`, the 303 behind `picked`. The skeptic keeps `cut`.
  - Can move golden granularity from whole page to region.
  - Undone by deleting the script, because the pages still navigate without it.
  - It collides with three things:
    - C16's lexical scan refuses the word `history` (`web/mod.rs:224-236`), and so `replaceState`. C16's own property (state rebuildable from the URL) does not need that.
    - The basis line sits in the head, outside the swapped region (`assets/page.html:7`).
    - C191.
- **(d) JSON documents and a small JS or TS client.**
  - The routes already serve their documents as JSON (`findings/gui.md:9`).
  - The browser-driving gate stage already exists (C187), so the test infrastructure is mostly paid for.
  - C16 as written already allows it: "the page is presentation". The retrospective says a TypeScript page over the Rust backend "is exactly what C46 permits" (`LANGUAGE-RETROSPECTIVE:660-663`).
  - Would delete most of `page.rs` and the 795 KB of HTML goldens.
  - The real obstacle: the pages print facts in words made at render time by `search_cmd` (`title`, `shown_title`, `ItemParts`; `page.rs:6-8`, `page.rs:2404-2414`). Those words would first have to move into the documents. Otherwise the client re-derives them, and that is the second semantics C46 forbids.
  - Plain JS with no bundler adds no packages. TypeScript adds a toolchain and a supply chain on the machine that holds the refresh token.
  - Tried cheaply on the item page, whose JSON is already 3.9 KB.

**Rulings that would change.** C191 is the owner's own law, in his words (`findings/gui.md:13`). C16's word list is a mechanism, and its pinned test is named in the ruling, so changing it is his too. C46 changes under none of (a) to (d), provided the words live in the documents.

**Position (judgment):** (c) now, behind one script, with goldens moved to regions. (b) only for escaping, if (c) is declined.

## H3: NARROWED

**Counted:**
- 21,486 tracked `.py` lines (`git ls-files '*.py' | xargs wc -l`).
  - 11,335 lines in 23 files under `tools/`; the largest is `trade-sheet.py` at 3,297, with 17 commits since 2026-10-25.
  - The rest are `search/*/scripts` and the seat skill.
- No type checker or linter is configured: no mypy, pyright or ruff in `tools/` or `.github`.
- Tests are `--self-test` and `--check` modes.

**What the gate runs:**
- `bothy.py` (the `bothy` stage).
- Eleven checks in `tools/trade-check.sh:54-60`, which run in the gate and in CI (`gate-linux.yml:141`).
- Python heredocs inside `docs-check.sh` and `gate.sh`.
- Roughly half the files run only by hand: `trade_rows`, `tracer-verify`, `site-listings`, `mutants-compare` and others.

**It is load-bearing.** Generators write the reference tables that `include_str!` compiles into the binary:
- `trade-stats-v1.toml` 1.36 MB, `trade-game-forms-v1.toml` 753 KB, `classes-v1.toml` 130 KB, `totals-v6.toml` 56 KB, `defences-v1.toml` 36 KB, plus the category and colour tables.
- Each file's header names its generator (`grep "Generated by"` over `crates/acquisition-search/reference/`).

**The gap.** `trade-check.sh:72-83` runs the class, defence and forms `--check`s only if `../poe1` exists, and CI checks out one repository. The skip prints `ok      clone ...` (`trade-check.sh:74`). It is deliberate and documented (C198, `trade-check.sh:28-37`), but it is the same shape as the audit's own issue `a-skipped-keyring-test-reads-as-a-pass`. A hand edit to 918 KB of shipped tables passes CI with only the drift line on their headers.

**A duplication.** The trade site's search-id codec (gzip, then base64url) exists twice: in Rust (`acquisition-search/src/trade.rs:166`, `:349`, `:450`) and in Python (`trade-sheet.py:58-85`, `trade_query.py`). Each is pinned to the captures separately; nothing tests one against the other.

**Position (judgment):** rightly Python; offline research over sibling clones does not belong under Cargo's compile bill. Two changes, though:
- The skip should print `skip`, not `ok`. A generated-table check is better made a stage that fails without the clone on the owner's machine.
- The Python encoder could be checked against the Rust one through the `acq` text output, or dropped in favour of it.

## What nobody named

1. **Escaping by convention over text from GGG.** `REVIEW-CHECKLIST.md` has no escaping row (`grep -in escap`), and no test pushes a hostile item, tab or note string through a page; the mock corpus has no `<`. Tab names, character names and item notes are typed by players. Judgment: mostly self-XSS, but on a page that holds the per-launch token and can POST an apply. This is the one concrete thing (b) would retire.
2. **The whole page as the unit of test.** `findings/gui.md:9` calls the markup disposable, but nobody has named the consequence. The 14 web goldens hold 76% of all golden bytes, and the gate's slowest stage carries them. Whatever happens to C191, golden granularity (whole page against region or document) is the choice that sets the cost per web change. No ruling or brief I read treats it as a choice.
