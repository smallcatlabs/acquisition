# Decisions — Daemon, jobs, protocol, accounts

Part of the decision registry (`AGENTS.md`, "Cross-cutting rulings"): the rulings that bind the daemon, the job model, the protocol, the client, sessions and accounts (`acquisition-daemon/src/daemon.rs` and the files under `daemon/`, `acquisition-client/src/client.rs`, `locator.rs`, `artifact.rs`, `acquisition-protocol/src/protocol.rs`, `job.rs`, `acquisition-store/src/jobs.rs`, `index.rs`). Read this before touching that code; the cross-cutting decisions and the invariants are in `AGENTS.md` and are always loaded. Same rules as there: one entry per ruling, in the form `tools/bothy.py`'s docstring states; the mechanism lives in the code's doc comments under the id.

## Daemon, jobs, protocol

- **C3 — The daemon spawns lazily and exits when idle.** A use verb spawns a daemon when none runs; an observation verb never spawns or replaces (C10, single-version handshake); a frontend spawns only where its own policy allows (C13, the MCP never in real mode). The daemon exits an hour (`ACQ_IDLE_SHUTDOWN`) after the last connection, submit or job's end, with none of those since and no queued work; a live job holds it however long the limiter makes it wait. A job verb with no login still spawns, and its refusal says that a daemon started and when it will exit. `daemon stop` and `daemon status` exist for debugging. *Why:* no stray processes and no start-up ceremony. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/idle.rs`, the `idle_verdict_` tests; `acquisition-cli/tests/readme_tour.rs`, the agent recipe.
- **C7 — A job's `params` travel on `JobInfo` as submitted, and they are public.** Every connected client sees them, so params never carry a secret; tokens are obtained inside the daemon, never passed in. *Why:* a job a person cannot identify cannot be managed, and rendering it is the client's business. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C127 — A client submits only the kinds the protocol's client list names, in the provider modes that list names, and every param that shapes a send is judged at submit.** Before an id exists the door refuses an unknown kind, the daemon's own `probe`, a mock-only kind in real mode, a path param missing where required, too long, or not one segment as any server reads it, a `sleep` beyond its bound, and params over theirs; no refusal quotes a client value whole. A path param renders as one encoded segment; a row the door would refuse fails in its words. *Why:* a client's string that reaches a URL or a timer unjudged can steer a send off the route its limiter key names, or panic a job already persisted. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/queue.rs`, the `c127_` tests.
- **C164 — A client's stash job for a tab the account's stored listing does not name is refused at submit.** With a listing of the job's realm and league stored, a tab it does not name, or names as gone, is refused in words that give the listing's age and say a `stashes` job fetches it again; a substash its parent tab's stored rows do not name, where they name any, is refused in words that give the age of the parent's fetch and say a `stash` job for the parent fetches its substashes again, since a `stashes` job keeps them; with no listing stored, the job is admitted. The daemon's own submissions never pass this door. *Why:* a request spent on a 404 the store already predicted is the API spent for nothing, and the daemon is the one door every client passes (C14); the cost is one listing fetch where a tab is newer than the stored listing. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/queue.rs`, `c164_a_stash_job_for_a_tab_the_stored_listing_does_not_name_is_refused_and_sends_nothing` and `c164_a_refused_substash_names_its_parents_fetch_which_lets_it_through`; the refusal through each frontend in `acquisition-cli/tests/realm_wire.rs` (golden `unlisted_tab`) and `acquisition-mcp/tests/tool_refusals.rs`, the `c164_` test.
- **C8 — The protocol has a subscribe channel from the start: job-state events over the same socket.** *Why:* GUI push updates and `jobs --watch` come nearly free, and adding a channel later is painful. *Pinned:* `acquisition-cli/tests/watch_recovery.rs`.
- **C9 — The protocol is JSON lines over a Unix socket or a named pipe.** *Why:* boring, debuggable and cross-platform. *Pinned:* `acquisition-protocol/tests/wire.rs`.
- **C85 — A connection carries one request in flight, and a subscription has a connection of its own: hello, subscribe, subscribed, then events only.** Events are invalidation hints, never a complete stream: a subscriber snapshots after subscribing and re-reads before relying on its view, and after `resync_required` or any disconnect it subscribes and snapshots again. A frame has a bound, and an oversize or malformed frame is answered with an error, not a closed socket. `hello` and `daemon_stop` are a stable plane every version parses and ignore an unknown field; the rest refuse one. *Why:* a client must see its losses to recover from them. *Pinned:* the `c85_` tests in `acquisition-protocol/tests/wire.rs` and `acquisition-client/tests/contract.rs`.
- **C10 — The handshake is single-version: a mismatched daemon is replaced, never negotiated with.** A client uses a daemon only when its provider and runtime identity (C84, contract and artifact) match what it would itself spawn; the identity changes with the code it governs and never derives from Git or a hand-kept value. A use verb may replace a mismatch; an observation verb never spawns or replaces, and reports absence and mismatch distinctly; an autonomous client (the MCP) never replaces. *Why:* a compatibility matrix is a reconciliation swamp, and an observer that replaced a daemon once cost a live run. *Pinned:* `acquisition-cli/tests/daemon_observe.rs`.
- **C84 — The runtime identity (C10, single-version handshake) is two values: the shared-contract revision, a digest of the protocol and store sources, manifests and lock, and the daemon artifact, the executable the daemon hashes at startup and a client compares with the sibling `acqd` it would spawn.** A mismatch on either is reported by name; a rebuilt `acqd` under a live daemon is an artifact mismatch; a daemon-only change moves only the artifact. One tree writes one artifact: the workspace's plain build and its test build resolve one feature graph and leave the same executables, so a test run never leaves a sibling behind the frontend that would spawn it. *Why:* the contract answers "can I use it" and the artifact "is it the one I would start", and each sees a failure the other cannot. *Pinned:* the `c84_` tests in `daemon_observe.rs` and, for what the MCP adds, `acquisition-mcp/tests/daemon_status.rs`; `tools/gate.sh` (the artifacts and features stages).
- **C83 — A world is a canonical, provider-neutral store root; its daemon holds an exclusive lock on it for its lifetime, and in real mode a per-OS-user lock no root bypasses.** A use path creates the root, then canonicalises and locks it; observation creates nothing. `hello` names the root, and a client refuses a daemon on another world; the endpoint is C112's (derived and private). Durable state (`daemon.db`, the rails state) lives in the world; the log and journal are bounded diagnostics elsewhere. *Why:* the persisted queue and the one-daemon bound were held by documentation, and the tripwire's state lived where the OS clears it at reboot. *Pinned:* `acquisition-cli/tests/world.rs`, the `c83_` tests.
- **C112 — The daemon's endpoint is derived from the world and the OS user, never chosen by hand.** Only that user can serve or reach it, and a client speaks to it only after verifying the server runs as that user. *Why:* a socket name is discovery, not ownership, and on Windows a named pipe's namespace is machine-wide, so privacy is the endpoint's property, not a directory's. *Pinned:* `acquisition-cli/tests/world.rs`.
- **C132 — The real-mode lock's path derives from the OS user alone, in the user's own data directory.** The home comes from the password database (on Windows, the profile directory from the process token), never from a variable; the lock sits in a private `run` directory beside the default store; the socket stays in the runtime directory (C112, the derived endpoint). Another user or mount namespace stays external concurrency (C31, one daemon, many sessions); a home that cannot hold the lock means no real-mode daemon, refused by name. *Why:* every temp directory is moved by a variable or age-cleaned under a held lock, and two locks are two gates. *Pinned:* `acquisition-store/unit/tests/world.rs`, `c83_the_lock_path_reads_no_environment`; `acquisition-cli/tests/world.rs`, the `c83_c31_` test.
- **C147 — A door and a ledger read take the world they act on; the environment is read once, at the frontend's construction.** `Client::connect`, `Client::observe`, `Persisted::observe` and `World::observe` take the world, and the environment-reading form calls them, kept for the binaries' doors; a `Frontend` passes its captured world to every call, so a frontend over a chosen world is expressible and nothing asserts two readings agree. A client keeps the child it spawns and reaps it, reports a failed stop on the replace path, and gives a child that exited on the world lock one more look for the winner (C86, the handshake deadline) before the spawn is called failed. *Why:* a long-lived frontend holds more than one world and outlives the daemons it spawns. *Pinned:* the `c147_` tests.
- **C137 — A deadline's contract is its behaviour at expiry, not the number: a test pins it by forcing expiry. No test asserts a wall-clock bound a slow machine can miss.** A liveness deadline widens through a named knob (`ACQ_HELLO_WITHIN`, `ACQ_STARTUP_WITHIN`, `ACQ_ANNOTATION_WAIT`, `ACQ_STORE_WAIT`, the idle exit's), set by the isolation list so a starved runner cannot fail a healthy path; the knob at zero forces the expiry. A new lifecycle that waits is designed as a machine whose time is an event (C157). *Why:* the Windows gate failed most first runs on deadlines measured against a starved runner, and each repriced budget only promoted the next margin. *Pinned:* the `c137_` tests in `acquisition-client/tests/contract.rs` and under `acquisition-store/unit/tests/` (the annotation file's open, the fact store's two opens and `ensure_wal`'s retry, the queue's open and its read-only opener).
- **C157 — A lifecycle that waits is a value: its state is data, its step a pure function of the state and one event returning the next state and its effects; time enters only as a named deadline's event and leaves only as a request to arm one; the socket, the process, the file system and the clock live in a driver that performs effects and returns events.** A machine is pinned by event scripts, one row per transition, in a synchronous test; a driver by one real-wiring test per effect kind under the hang ceiling, asserting no duration; a cross-process test asserts order and outcome. A wait enters a machine when its ordering against another event is a decision of the lifecycle, or when its only pin asserts elapsed time; a wait whose interleavings are already forced without relying on elapsed time, and whose outcome the lifecycle takes as one value, stays a driver effect with its bound inside. *Why:* a deadline tested in real time is a bet that the machine is healthy, and the Windows gate lost that bet most first runs; a deadline that is a row cannot be late. *Pinned:* `c157_the_door_is_a_table_of_event_scripts` in `acquisition-client/src/door.rs` (the `Door` table) and `c157_the_lifecycle_is_a_table_of_event_scripts` in `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/exit.rs` (the `Life` table).
- **C158 — A daemon scenario crosses the daemon's frontend boundary only, and its time moves by its own hand or when nothing it started can run.** It enters through the protocol and reads the journal and the protocol's answers; it moves its clock explicitly or lets the runtime move it, never a poll; a test that needs another boundary (several workers, a child process, the daemon's own socket, a function or a table) says which and stays a component test. *Why:* a test that drives internals pins this build's mechanics and dies with a rewrite; a count of waiting tasks is wrong once two park, and the runtime's own park is the one exact signal. *Pinned:* `c158_the_wire_table_through_the_door` and `c158_a_blocking_task_holds_the_clock_until_the_runtime_observes_its_end` in `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/dispatch.rs`; check 10 of `tools/arch-check.sh` (one `spawn_blocking`, the counted helper's).
- **C82 — At most one installation targets a world.** The installation places one `acqd` beside every frontend, and a frontend spawns only the sibling of its own canonical executable: no `PATH`, no configured path, no embedded mode; no sibling, no spawn, reported. Tests and drivers locate it the same way. A second installation targeting one world is a design event. *Why:* C10's "the runtime it would itself spawn" (the handshake's identity) must name one file, or two installations thrash. *Pinned:* `acquisition-client/src/locator.rs`, the `c82_` test; `tools/release-smoke.sh`.
- **C27 — A client that disappears leaves its jobs running.** Ctrl-C, a closed terminal or a crash cancels nothing. The daemon runs in a process group of its own on every OS. A client reattaches by job id (`acq result <id>`), answered across daemon restarts. *Why:* the sends are committed either way. *Pinned:* `acquisition-client/tests/contract.rs`; `acquisition-cli/tests/provider_default.rs`, `a_lazily_started_daemon_survives_a_hangup_or_a_ctrl_c_at_the_prompt_that_started_it`.
- **C133 — The daemon has one exit, and every cause reaches it.** `daemon stop`, the idle verdict and a terminating signal run one path: submits are refused as stopping; a job parked before its send returns to waiting (C25); the jobs past admission, the token refreshes and a pending login have 15 s of wall-clock time to land and be recorded; then the logs are checkpointed, the socket removed and the process exits. At the bound the drain ends; a second signal, or an exit not gone 3 s past the bound, ends the process at once, leaving what it cuts running on disk for C6 to replay. A pending login holds the idle verdict. *Why:* `stop` or a replace by a newer frontend could cut a refresh in flight and lose its rotated token and journal line. *Pinned:* `exit.rs`.
- **C134 — A broken internal invariant ends the process; a listener failure never does.** A panic is logged through a handle that takes no lock, a marker names the job it ran in, and the process aborts; the next lifetime fails that job as faulted instead of replaying it (C6), and a probe's marker also closes its route for the cooldown, failing the jobs that would recreate it, so a body that breaks the daemon is sent once. An error from `accept` is logged, with a backoff, and served through; an unreachable daemon idles out (C3). *Why:* a caught task panic left a stray that never idled out, and one accept error dropped the world lock with sends in flight. *Pinned:* `exit.rs`, `a_fault_marker_fails_its_job_once`, `a_listener_error_is_served_through`, `a_faulted_probe_closes_its_route`.
- **C123 — The stand-in provider serves real-shaped items from a committed fixture.** Its bodies are the owner's own items, exported from his store under his grant and chosen for the traits the journey needs, or hand-written to the census (`search/DIGEST.md`) where his store holds none; compiled into `acqd`, never a path read at run time; never a body from the trade site or another player. *Why:* a user, a seat and the journey test meet search and price through fetching, on bodies whose shape is the API's today, and the fixture's provenance stays the owner's and the project's. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/mockggg.rs`, `every_fixture_item_`; `acquisition-cli/tests/goldens/journey/a_first_session.txt` and its twin `acquisition-mcp/tests/journey.rs`.

The owner's grant covers items from any of his stores, so a trait the fixture lacks is taken from one of them before it is hand-written.

## Accounts and sessions

- **C31 — Multi-account is one daemon holding many sessions, not a daemon per account.** The Cloudflare bound is per IP and held as per-process state, so two daemons on one machine make a burst neither sees; the world lock (C83, one daemon per world) holds it among one OS user's real-mode daemons, and other users and processes stay external concurrency. Limiter and probe state is keyed per `(account, policy)` for `Account` rules. *Why:* GGG counts `Account` rules per account. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/ratelimit/tests/spec.rs`, `account_rules_are_paced_per_account`.
- **C50 — The stable account key is the profile `uuid`, fetched at login and required.** A login whose profile fetch fails fails whole: no provisional identity, no minted keys, no rename repair. The fetch belongs to its login's lifetime: a later lifetime fails it at restore and sends nothing for it, and the user logs in again. `accounts.json` maps username, discriminator and provider to the uuid; fact files are named by username (refetchable), annotation files by uuid. *Why:* intent references identity, and an unstable key is what the C++ app's legacy buyouts paid for. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/login.rs`, `a_login_whose_profile_fetch_fails_fails_whole`; `queue.rs`, `c50_a_lost_logins_profile_fetch_fails_at_restore_and_sends_nothing`.
- **C51 — The daemon keeps no default account; selection is stateless.** Every submit carries `account`; omitted, it resolves only when exactly one session exists, and otherwise the daemon refuses with the list. The CLI resolves `--account` against `accounts.json` client-side, exactly and never by prefix, so reads never spawn a daemon, and among several known accounts a job names its account, refused by the frontend before it submits. A job has exactly one account; cross-account work is a frontend loop. *Why:* a default held in the daemon is state every client would have to guess. *Pinned:* `acquisition-client/tests/contract.rs`; the job's clause by `acquisition-verbs/tests/documents.rs` (`c51_a_job_names_its_account_and_among_several_none_named_is_refused`) and `acquisition-cli/tests/seat_faults.rs`.
- **C135 — The access token is kept in the keyring beside the refresh token and restored with it; a lifetime refreshes only at expiry.** The entry is one JSON object the daemon alone writes and reads; a bare token, an entry an earlier daemon saved, restores as before and is rewritten at its first refresh; an earlier daemon reading the new entry meets a dead grant (C24) and logs in again. *Why:* one token POST per lifetime was one per minute of idle cycling. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/refresh.rs`, `a_restored_session_sends_no_refresh_before_expiry`.
- **C136 — The submitter `daemon` is the daemon's own: a client submit that names it is refused at the door.** *Why:* a rule keyed on the daemon's own work, as C50's lost login is, must rest on a mark no client can forge. *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/queue.rs`, `c136_a_client_submit_as_the_daemon_is_refused_and_sends_nothing`.
- **C144 — The daemon reports no success it did not earn.** A store that cannot be opened or written after a send fails the job, and the failure carries the fetched body as an optional payload so the send is not wasted; every credential-store result reaches the caller and the words the user reads, so logout and forget say what the keyring did, and a rename or a failed save clears the entry it leaves behind; a staged login token serves the login's own profile job alone (C136, the daemon's own submitter); the account index is written atomically, synced, under the one lock. *Why:* a success the user is told and the tree does not hold costs a session or a secret, and it is the fault every review keeps finding in prose. *Pinned:* the `c144_` tests in the daemon, store, protocol, CLI.
- **C148 — No credential-store or account-index I/O runs under the daemon's shared lock, and `hello` is answered under no lock such I/O can hold.** A refresh, a login, a logout and a forget change the session table under `shared` and do their keyring and index I/O under one credentials lock, on a blocking thread, publishing the result under `shared` after; the start's restore is the fifth landing: the socket serves before it, a request that needs a session waits for it, and the dispatcher, the idle verdict and the exit's drain hold for it; a waiter on a refresh and a bearer read under a permit are served at the install, never the save; a login being recorded refuses a second; the exit's drain waits for a save in flight to its bound. *Why:* a keychain prompt under `shared` made every client report the daemon unresponsive and held jobs behind their token read (#81). *Pinned:* `acquisition-daemon/src/daemon/tests/credentials.rs`, the `c148_` tests.

## Not doing

What someone would propose and the project declines, each until the trigger it names or for good; the backlog is the repository's GitHub issues.

- Packaging beyond the GitHub archives (an installer, Windows signing, notarization, another channel): not until the owner reopens a channel, or a user is stopped by Gatekeeper on a browser-fetched macOS archive or by SmartScreen on a Windows one.
- A second installation targeting one world: refused under C82; a consumer that needs one is a design event.
- keyring 4's per-platform stores in place of keyring 3's backends: not until a sandboxed package or a provisioning-profile bundle.
- A GUI-hosted daemon: refused; the GUI spawns `acqd` as the CLI does, and phase 4 records the spawn policy.
- musl static Linux builds below the glibc 2.35 floor: not until a user on an older distribution.
- An allowlist of GGG's policy and rule names in the daemon log: not until there is a demonstrated need (the owner, 2026-10-29, daemon-safety's close).
- Keeping GGG's text out of the send journal: refused; the journal keeps GGG's rate and header text verbatim, and invariant 5 is not touched, because no token reaches it (the owner, 2026-10-29).
- Typing GGG's text where it enters the daemon (#132): not until a need or value is identified (the owner, 2026-10-29).
- Replacing GGG's policy and rule names with the daemon's labels on `daemon status`, the dashboard and the diagnosis (#133): not until #132's need; the names stay as GGG sent them, and the diagnosis redactor is their belt (the owner, 2026-10-30).
- A classification field in `Outcome` (why a job failed, as data): not until a frontend asks for it (the owner, 2026-10-31, t5's design).
- The choke point's admission loop as a machine, a `Halted` event in place of its one-second slices: not until a live run shows a halt seen late by a task parked in a hold, or the slices cost a measured amount; the slices are what keep a halt prompt and cost nothing under the door (the owner, 2026-11-01, the retrospective's follow-up). Classification stays a component property of the daemon; a field in `Outcome` would change a user's JSON, serialized and persisted.
- A failed retention prune at the restore stopping the queue: refused; the prune deletes only terminal rows memory never holds, so its failure leaves old rows on disk longer and no job that memory and disk disagree on, and it is logged (C6's sticky rule covers the restore's probe delete, not the prune).
