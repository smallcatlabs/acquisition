# Decisions — Process: who decides, and how a step runs

Part of the decision registry (`AGENTS.md`, "Cross-cutting rulings", whose rules apply unchanged): the rulings that bind how the work runs — who decides what, how a step is briefed, built, reviewed and closed, where the state of the road is kept, and what an agent's memory may hold — in `roadmap/README.md`, `roadmap/briefs/TEMPLATE.md`, `roadmap/briefs/REVIEW-PROMPT.md`, `roadmap/briefs/BRIEF-REVIEW-PROMPT.md`, `tools/gate.sh`, `.githooks` and `.claude/skills`. Read this before starting a step, closing one, ordering a review or changing a skill. The mechanics are in the skills (session-close, cold-review) and in the brief template; the workflows' own rulings are `decisions/ci.md`.

## Who decides

- **C170 — The owner rules on his classes, and a session decides everything else and records it.** His classes are an invariant, a boundary, the words of this registry, the gate's stages, and anything that touches his real store or the live API. Agent-facing design (a verb, a flag, an MCP tool, a query word, the words of an answer) is the session's, recorded in the commit that builds it. A choice in his classes that has one clear answer may be taken under his delegation, and it then goes to his queue (C179, one queue for his word). A choice with two defensible answers waits for him. *Why:* he would rather be asked than find a boundary moved, and he does not want to be asked what a session can settle. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C171 — A session commits to `main` when the gate exits 0 and pushes when a unit of work is committed and gated, without asking.** One concern is one commit, straight to `main` with no branch. A commit lands before each time the owner looks, so his input shows as a before and an after. A push stands alone in its command, and one the harness refuses is left and reported, never worked around. *Why:* the gate already judges a commit, and asking for each one added a wait and no check. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C186 — `main` takes no commit whose committer is Claude: a cloud session's commits are committed again on the Mac, the owner as committer, before they reach it.** The pre-push hook refuses a push to `main` that carries one, names each, and prints how to commit them again; the author, the message and its `Assisted-by:` trailer stay as written. No server-side rule refuses one, since GitHub's free plan offers none; a CI run on each push to `main` reports one as a red run, by the hook's own test of a committer. *Why:* every commit on `main` is committed and signed by the owner's own key, and a rule a session's memory held alone was a reading, not structure (C182, structure before a rule). *Pinned:* `.githooks/pre-push`, `tools/committer-check.sh`, `tools/githooks-breakers.sh`.
- **C179 — A decision in the owner's classes that a session needs or has taken is an issue labelled `trigger:ruling`, and no record promises him a later read.** A question that blocks work says what waits on it. A decision taken under his delegation says what was decided and why, the work goes on, and he closes the issue or reverses it. One issue holds one decision. Each close says how many are waiting. *Why:* a question that waited on him was answered within days, and a list left for a later read was not read. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C181 — A milestone's date is advisory, and no engineering is cut to meet it.** Work that closes early pulls the next item forward. *Why:* the owner would rather do good work on the project's own timeline than compromise it for a deadline. *Pinned:* nothing yet.

## A step

- **C172 — Opus plans and builds, Fable designs and advises, and the owner rules, runs anything live and sits at the site.** Design is choosing a shape where more than one is defensible: a boundary, a seam, the form of a document or of the protocol. Planning is how a chosen shape lands: its steps, its brief, its done-when. When a plan or a build turns up a choice of shape, that one choice goes to Fable and the rest carries on. *Why:* the cheaper model plans and builds well once the shape is chosen. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C185 — Builds run one at a time in one tree, and a measurement runs alone on the machine.** No build, gate or measurement runs while the owner sits at the machine in a window he names. *Why:* two builds in one tree mismatch their artifacts (C84, the artifact mismatch), and a second load changes what a measurement measures. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C173 — A step is an issue or a brief, a build, the gate, its commits, a cold review and a close.** A small fix needs no brief, since its issue states the goal and the done-when. A step that needs a design has a design brief first, which the owner rules before anything is built. A review's finding is fixed by one rule and pinned by a test, never patched case by case. A step that has not met its done-when slips rather than ships. *Why:* the same six moves hold a one-line fix and a month's design, so a session never has to ask which process applies. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C174 — A brief carries rules and commands, never their output.** It states the goal, the fixed inputs, the decisions that needed thought, the done-when and what is out of scope. Where a step must find every instance of something, the brief names the command that finds them, and the builder runs it first and reports what it found. A count or a claim about the tree stands in a brief only beside the command that produced it. A brief over 8,000 bytes is carrying the tree or is two steps. *Why:* a brief's account of the tree goes stale before the build, and most findings against briefs were against that account. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C178 — A step's close is one commit whose message is the step's record, found by its `Step:` trailer.** The message checks the done-when against the tree, answers each item the builder raised, and names what was retired and what was left open with its home. `git log --grep='^Step: '` is the ledger, and no file retells it. The same commit rewrites the road's state (C180, the state file). *Why:* a close retold in a ledger row, a status paragraph and a history line was three more claims to keep true. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C180 — `roadmap/README.md` says what is happening now, what is next and what only the owner can do, and each close rewrites it.** It holds no history, because git holds what it said before. "Next" is in order, and a session takes its first item without a planning session. The file stays under a byte cap that fails the gate. *Why:* a file that is read first and only appended to buried its one current sentence under everything it had ever said. *Pinned:* `tools/docs-check.sh` (the state file's cap), `tools/docs-check-breakers.sh`.
- **C184 — A commit made from an agent's shell names its assistant in an `Assisted-by:` trailer.** The message ends with `Co-Authored-By: Claude <model> <noreply@anthropic.com>`, which the commit-msg hook rewrites. The hook refuses an agent's commit that names none and prints the line to add. *Why:* disclosure that depended on each session remembering a line was forgotten more than once. *Pinned:* `.githooks/commit-msg`, `.githooks/prepare-commit-msg`, `tools/githooks-breakers.sh`.

## Review

- **C175 — A round that changes behaviour, a tool, the gate, a workflow or the shared test harness is reviewed cold before its step closes.** A round that changes only tests gets its retirement lines and the gate. A round that changes only comments and documents gets no review, unless it cuts or moves prose across many files. The reviewer reads the commit against its brief or its issue and is never given the builder's report. *Why:* each reviewed build found a real fault, and the rounds left unreviewed let through none found later. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C176 — A build gets at most three cold reviews on a session's judgment, a brief two, and a spike's brief one.** A round is followed by another only when it found something that changes what gets built. A brief whose last round still finds such faults is answered and built, with its build's review as the check (C175, what is reviewed). A build's third round that still finds them stops the step for the owner. A review past these counts is the owner's word, quoted in the commit that saves it. *Why:* a brief's later rounds found new faults in each fix's detail and did not converge, and a build's review found what no reading of a brief could. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C177 — A cold review is run by a model outside the author's family where one is installed, from a standing prompt.** The session that made the commit launches it and adds only the commit and its brief's name or its issue's own text. A review run by a model of the author's family says so in its first line. The answer is saved whole under `roadmap/reviews/` and committed before the round that answers it. *Why:* a reviewer of the author's family shares its blind spots, and a prompt the author writes hands the reviewer the author's reading. *Pinned:* nothing yet.

## The rules themselves

- **C182 — A rule, a checklist row or a template line is added only when no structure can hold the fault, and is retired by a commit naming what holds it now.** A fault first becomes structure: a type, a dependency edge, a check, or a refusal that says what to do. A rule that an existing line covers joins that line. The retirement line is C154's (the retirement rule), and a phase close reads the process text for what can now retire. *Why:* the retirement rule emptied nothing but tests, while every incident added a line somewhere. *Pinned:* nothing yet.
- **C183 — A rule about how the project runs lives in the tree, and an agent's private memory holds only tool traps, pointers and the owner's preferences.** Where memory and the tree disagree, the tree governs. A session that learns a project rule writes it to the tree in the same turn. *Why:* a private rule is invisible to the owner, to a reviewer and to a session on another machine. *Pinned:* nothing yet.

## Not doing

What someone would propose and the project declines, each until the trigger it names or for good; the backlog is the repository's GitHub issues.

- A gate check that counts the reviews of an artifact: not until a review past its count is saved without the owner's quoted word.
- A byte budget on `AGENTS.md` or on a registry entry: refused; the count of cross-cutting rulings and the entry's shape hold what the bytes stood in for.
- A ledger file of closed steps: refused; the close commits are the ledger, and `roadmap/LEDGER.md` stands as written to 2026-11-09.
- Deleting or folding `roadmap/reviews/` and `roadmap/briefs/`: not until a reader is slowed by them; they sit on no reading path.
- A skill for driving workers across sessions: not until a second harness needs the procedure; one harness's traps stay in the agent's memory (C183, where a rule lives).
