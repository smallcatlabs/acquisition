# Decisions — Item search: the crate, the line, the query, the answer, the basis

Part of the decision registry (`CONTEXT.md`, "Decisions — the registry", whose rules apply unchanged): the rulings that bind item search and the `acquisition-search` crate; the trade boundary's are `decisions/trade.md`. Read this before touching `search/` or the crate, with `search/README.md` (the slice's working directory, its rules and traps) and `search/DIGEST.md` (the `S` ids cited here, looked up by id and never read whole).

**Standing.** Every line stands: it changes by the owner's ruling and by nothing else, and the commit that changes it quotes him; a line found wanting is brought to him. The language on one page is `acq search --describe`; how the lines were reached is `search/LEDGER.md`, "How the design was reached"; his words on each line and the dated trail are this file's history (`git log -p decisions/search.md`).

## The crate and the deriver (ruled)

- **C89 — Item search is its own library crate, `acquisition-search`, over the store's read API.** It links the store, and `acquisition-plan` once a query reads the effective price (C81, the effective price); never the daemon, the client, an HTTP client or an async runtime, and it opens no file of its own, so it is no third door (C12, two surfaces). The daemon and the planner never link it: a plan takes item ids, never a query. *Why:* each property is an edge the check can refuse, and a search edit recompiles no daemon. *Pinned:* `tools/docs-check.sh` (the edges).
- **C103 — One deriver per fact.** Place and every other ingest fact come from the store's read, joined as the store's own reads join them, never re-derived from a body; what a body says is derived in the search crate by a pure function of the body and those facts that names no store type, so it can move under the store unchanged. What the store gains for it is C108 (the corpus read). *Why:* two reads in one crate already meant different things by "in this league" (S131, two readings of place), and two derivers over one body is that bug across a crate line. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/derive.rs`.

## The line and the query

- **C90 — A line's identity is (realm, kind, template), and the displayed text is the only name a line needs.** The template is the displayed English with each number replaced by `#`, markup reduced to its display half and the sign carried in the number; numbers are ordered slots, never averaged. Kind keeps both coordinates the body gives, the source array and the line's flags (`crafted`, `fractured`, `mutated`), so neither bucket rule is chosen silently. A rename is a new line. A stat id exists only at the trade boundary (C99, the translation). *Why:* the template names every line and the stat id two thirds of them (S155, lines without a stat id). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/derive.rs`.
- **C91 — A query is one typed tree and a value: immutable, serializable, handed between a human and an agent, to the site and from it.** And, or, not and at-least-N-of (with a lower and an upper bound) compose across every field, line and place alike, and every surface encodes the one tree. A refinement is the old query, parenthesised, and a new term. There are no silent modes: an ambiguity the grammar defines is an error that shows the readings, and everything else follows one visible rule the canonical query prints back. *Why:* the C++ app cannot compose and the site composes only among stats (S169, the owner's boolean query), and an answer must say how it was understood. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/language.rs`.
- **C104 — The text is carried on every surface; the tree is accepted and always returned.** The canonical text and the canonical tree are one value, each printing to the other; the CLI and the MCP pass the text, a client that holds a tree (a GUI's rows) may send it, and every answer carries both. *Why:* both reviewers wrote text, and a refinement stays the previous query plus one term (S195, refinement composes). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/language.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/answer.rs`.
- **C109 — A query nests at most 32 deep, as text or as a tree; deeper is refused as `too_deep`.** A level is each `( )`, `not`, `holds( )` or `undecided( )` around a term, inside a line's group as outside it; `line( )`, `linked( )` and `sum( )` are terms, not levels. *Why:* a recursive reader aborts the process past what the stack holds (C47, no panic on input), and the limit sits far under the shallowest abort and far over any query asked. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/depth.rs`.
- **C92 — A comparison on a line binds one occurrence; the item's total is asked for by name.** `sum` of the line, or a named total (C94, named totals), is the item's sum. Conditions on one line are one term and hold on one occurrence. Only a ranged line (`# to #`) names its numbers `low` and `high` and admits `avg`; a comparison, sort or sum naming no slot on a line with several is an error that lists them. A row shows the line whole; an item with no qualifying line sorts last; the answer counts the items whose lines reach a lower bound only together. *Why:* a sum is built from occurrences by a word, and an occurrence cannot be recovered from a sum. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/rows.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/ranged.rs`.
- **C93 — Absent is false; undecided is one outcome with a named reason; the two are never one count.** A comparison on something the item lacks is false, never zero; absence is claimed only when everything that could hold the thing was readable. What cannot be established on an item (an unread body, a base the class table lacks, an unresolved price) makes only the terms that needed it undecided; composition carries it, and an item undecided at the root is no match, counted apart. Per atomic term the answer counts matched, failed, lacked and undecided. *Why:* an unknown count nonzero on every ordinary query stops being read; one nearly always zero is read when it is not. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/unread.rs`.
- **C94 — A named total is a sum that answers, defined once as reviewed reference data (C68, reference tables).** A row names the contributing line (template, kind, realm, slot, item category) and its weight; a row with a weight and no slot counts the line; a total over ranged lines is a range, summed slot-wise, and takes `low`, `high` and `avg`. Nothing else defines a total. An unreadable contributor leaves a subtotal marked incomplete, never a total, and no recipe for a realm is never a zero. The answer prints a definition on request, and the table is checked loudly when built. *Why:* a total that may decline fails the everyday question. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/pseudo.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/ranged.rs`.
- **C95 — The counts view has two shapes and one optional sum; there is no aggregation language.** Several names give independent facets, or one crossed table of two fields, never `line` (C97, the vocabulary read). A count may carry one sum: lacking adds nothing and is counted, empty is zero, unread leaves an incomplete subtotal (C94, named totals). Summing raw line projections, combining views, sorting counts and a standalone sum are errors, and there is no grand total. *Why:* counts and totals are asked for; an aggregation language and an implicit largest occurrence are not. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/counts.rs`.
- **C105 — A count accounts for every matching item.** Known absence is `none`; a key not established is `undecided` (C93, absent versus undecided), neither omitted nor merged with a value, and undecided in aggregate carries no reason. Every match is in at least one real bucket, `none` or `undecided`; a one-value key's buckets sum exactly to the total. *Why:* inventing a value or dropping an item breaks C93 or the total. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/counts.rs`.
- **C96 — Place is terms, but the realm is the search's scope, never a term, and a query never spans realms by default.** League, tab, character and container are terms; which realm a search runs over is stated outside the conditions, printed back in every answer, and never hoisted from a branch of the tree. Over a store holding more than one realm, a query naming none is an error that lists them, and an explicit all-realms scope exists. Within a realm the default is every league, live items. The scope block says what was searched: items, realms, leagues, locations never fetched, the oldest fetch. *Why:* a template and a total mean different things in two games (C58, realm above league). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/answer.rs`.
- **C97 — The vocabulary is a read of this corpus and the same call as search.** Counting by `line` under the query in hand lists the templates the matching items carry, by kind (and by realm under an all-realms scope), ranked by how many items carry each, with the range per slot; several texts may be asked in one call, and every row carries the term that selects it, ready to paste. The closed lists (fields, kinds, flags, totals with their definitions) are printed in the help. Autocomplete and an agent's schema discovery are this one read. *Why:* a human's autocomplete and an agent's discovery are one need. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/counts.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/rows.rs`.

## The corpus and the answer

- **C98 — No search service: a consumer holds the corpus, and every answer is derived from and labelled with one consistent basis.** The basis names the store and account, one snapshot of facts with locations, the intent revision, the build, and the reference and derivation versions; a held corpus is reused only while every component it carries matches the basis of the answer in hand, checked before every answer. A write after the basis is chosen does not unmake a correctly labelled answer. A persisted projection is a candidate that waits for a measured failure. *Why:* a check is structure where an event is discipline (P5, structure over discipline), and a stale result taken for truth is C48's failure (no cached search). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/answer.rs`.
- **C100 — There is one answer shape: the canonical query, basis, scope, terms block, total, a view and how to continue.** A row is compact by default (id, name, base, rarity, place by name and id, the lines the query touched with their values; C53, the decision view), and the caller may name fields; the body comes only through `show` (`--against`: the why-not). Every printed id is accepted back. A continuation names its basis (C98, one basis) and the query, view and order it continues, and is refused across a change. `priced` is the effective price (C81, the most specific statement), joined read-only. *Why:* an answer that carries its evidence needs no second call and no body. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/rows.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/next.rs`.
- **C101 — A missing reach is a field to add, never a general door.** There is no raw-path accessor over the body and no general collection syntax. Socket colours are asked over the whole item and within one link group. A pseudo that is not a sum or count over lines is a derived field, a named function over properties, lines and reference data (C68, reference tables), listed with its definition, never a total row (C94, named totals). Unmapped site filters stay an open catalogue, listed as gaps. A change to what a user types is recorded as a ruling before the code, under the owner's delegation. *Why:* reach is a property of the derivation, and a derived field is the cheap class of change. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/percentile.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/sockets.rs`.
- **C102 — The digest's limits register is inherited with one exception, and each limit is an output.** What the search says on meeting a limit is the register's wording (`search/DIGEST.md`, "Limits register"); the exception is a moved template under a shipped total, which looks like a lacking one (C94, named totals). Binding occurrences does not say which twin is local; a site defence bound is inexact (C99, the translation); judgment of what is good, worth keeping or legacy is the user's (C106, no judgment). The search never fuzzy-matches, values, judges legacy, fetches, acts on the stash, or recovers the mod behind a line. *Why:* a limit stated at the answer is read; one in a document is not. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/refusal.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/acceptance.rs`.
- **C106 — Reference data admits game data and community convention, never judgment.** Data is what the game files state (base ranges); convention is a concept with a published community definition (equipment, the trade site's categories). Both enter as reviewed tables (C68) from a registered surface (C79, governed surfaces), through the admission test the crate's doc states, and remove nothing: the user composes their own meaning from terms. Judgment (what is good, what it is worth, what to keep) is never embedded, and legacy stays the user's (C107, legacy is the user's). *Why:* a derived field needs data under it, and judgment embedded there would be the tool's, not the user's. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/class.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/category.rs`.
- **C107 — Legacy is the user's word; a source's own label may be a field.** No field, term or output of the search says "legacy". What a registered source prints about an item, such as the variant label Path of Building gives a unique (`Pre 3.23.0`, `Current`), is convention under C106 (data and convention) and may back a derived field (C101, fields not doors) that prints the label and the source's version; an item that fits an old and a current variant, or none, is undecided with its reason (C93, absent versus undecided). *Why:* a source exists for a unique's variants, and none, for any item, says what the game can still produce. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/acceptance.rs`.
- **C117 — GGG's facts about a tab are fields, and the tool says nothing of what the site lists.** `tab.public` (the listing's `metadata.public`; a substash takes its tab's; an item on a character lacks it), `tab.remove_only` (the name suffix GGG adds, N47) and `price.side` are searchable. Whether the site would list an item is the agent's inference, labelled as such. *Why:* seat runs inferred "would a buyer see it" from `has:priced` and `price show` while the store held the flag. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/seat_faults.rs`, `acquisition-cli/tests/seat_faults.rs`.
- **C118 — An answer says how old a row is and what kind of place it could not see.** A row carries its location's fetch age beside `place` (text in the CLI, an epoch in JSON; C53, three output levels); the scope line says the never fetched by tab type, the largest named and the rest counted, and points at the list that exists. Why a location was never fetched, by policy or not yet, is left out until a seat asks. *Why:* a map question got the same flat line as a ring question over a mostly unfetched map stash, and "where do I pick it up" needs the age. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/seat_faults.rs`, `acquisition-search/tests/explain.rs`.
