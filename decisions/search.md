## The crate and the deriver (ruled)

- **C89 — Item search is its own library crate, `acquisition-search`, over the store's read API.** It links the store, and `acquisition-plan` once a query reads the effective price (C81); never the daemon, the client, an HTTP client or an async runtime, and it opens no file of its own, so it is no third door (C12): a frontend links it as it links the planner (C1). The daemon and the planner never link it — a plan takes item ids, never a query. *Why:* each property is an edge the check can refuse, and a search edit recompiles no daemon. Ruled 2026-09-17 (owner: "Agreed with a new crate"; the edges: "I agree to 1 entirely").
- **C103 — One deriver per fact.** Place and every other ingest fact come from the store's read, joined as the store's own reads join them (S131), never re-derived from a body; what a body says is derived in the search crate, by a pure function of the body and those facts that names no store type, so it can move under the store unchanged if persistence fires (C98). What the store gains for it is C108 (`decisions/store.md`). *Why:* two reads in one crate already mean different things by "in this league" (S131); two derivers over one body is that bug with a crate line through it. *Evidence:* S127, S128, S131. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/derive.rs`. Ruled 2026-09-17 ("I agree to 1 entirely").

## The line and the query

- **C90 — A line's identity is (realm, kind, template), and the displayed text is the only name a line needs.** The template is the displayed English with each number replaced by `#`, markup reduced to its display half and the sign carried in the number; numbers are ordered slots, never averaged. Kind keeps both coordinates the body gives — the source array and the flags the line carries (`crafted`, `fractured`, `mutated`; S17) — so neither bucket rule is chosen silently. A rename is a new line. A stat id exists only at the trade boundary (C99). *Why:* the template names every line and the stat id two thirds (S155); the text is the moving part. *Evidence:* S3, S4, S17, S68, S71. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/derive.rs`. 2026-09-17.
- **C91 — A query is one typed tree, and a value: immutable, serializable, handed between a human and an agent, to the site and from it.** And, or, not and at-least-N-of (with a lower and an upper bound) compose across every field, line and place alike, and every surface is an encoding of the one tree. A refinement is the old query, parenthesised, and a new term. There are no silent modes: an ambiguity the grammar defines is an error that shows the readings, and everything else follows one visible rule that the canonical query prints back. *Why:* composition the C++ app lacks and the site allows only among stats (S169); an answer that says how it was understood is what both designs reached unprompted. *Evidence:* S169, S172. 2026-09-17.
- **C104 — The text is carried on every seat; the tree is accepted and always returned.** The canonical text and the canonical tree are one value, each printing to the other; the CLI and the MCP pass the text, a client that holds a tree (a GUI's rows) may send it, and every answer carries both. *Why:* both reviewers wrote text in both designs; that the string is the shorter form is a judgment, not a measurement. It departs from the letter of R14 — "a query object, not a text argument" (S195) — and keeps its intent, a refinement being the previous query plus one term. No review compared the text with an equally specified JSON, an adapter choice, kept at the first seat (owner, 2026-09-26: "keeping as is sounds appropriate"). *Evidence:* S195. 2026-09-17.
- **C109 — A query nests at most 32 deep, as text or as a tree; deeper is refused as `too_deep`.** A level is each `( )`, `not`, `holds( )` or `undecided( )` around a term, inside a line's group as outside it; `line( )`, `linked( )` and `sum( )` are terms, not levels. *Why:* recursive readers abort the process past what the stack holds (C47); the limit sits far under the shallowest abort measured (102) and far over any query asked (3). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/depth.rs`. 2026-10-11.
- **C92 — A comparison on a line binds one occurrence; the item's total is asked for by name.** `sum` of the line, or a named total (C94), is the item's sum. Conditions on one line are one term and hold on one occurrence. A ranged line (`# to #`) names its numbers `low` and `high` and admits `avg`; a comparison, sort or sum naming no slot on a line with several is an error that lists them. A row shows the line whole; an item with no qualifying line sorts last; the answer counts the items whose lines reach a lower bound only together. *Why:* a sum is built from occurrences by a word; an occurrence cannot be recovered from a sum. *Evidence:* S27, S28, S47, S68. Owner 2026-09-17: "low and high are better than min and max, and this only applies to ranged modifiers".
- **C93 — Absent is false; undecided is one outcome with a named reason, and the two are never one count.** A comparison on something the item lacks is false, never zero (S158); absence is claimed only when everything that could hold the thing was readable. What cannot be established on an item — an unread body, a base the class table lacks, an unresolved price (C81) — makes only the terms that needed it undecided; composition carries it, and an item undecided at the root is no match, counted apart. Per atomic term the answer counts matched, failed, lacked, undecided. *Why:* an unknown count that is nonzero on every ordinary query stops being read; one that is nearly always zero is read when it is not. *Evidence:* S107, S158. Owner 2026-09-17: "accept".
- **C94 — A named total is a sum that answers, defined once as reviewed reference data (C68).** A row names the contributing line — template, kind, realm, slot, item category — and its weight; a row with a weight and no slot counts the line; a total over ranged lines is a range, summed slot-wise, and takes `low`, `high` and `avg`. Nothing else defines a total, so it has one meaning in every surface; an unreadable contributor leaves a subtotal marked incomplete, never a total, and no recipe for a realm is never a zero. The answer prints a definition on request; a definition is checked, loudly, when the table is built. *Why:* a total that may decline failed the everyday question in use (stance 3). *Evidence:* S29, S49, S111. Owner 2026-09-17: "yes, accepted". *Details:* `totals.rs`.
- **C95 — The counts view has two shapes and one optional sum; there is no aggregation language.** Several names give independent facets (AQ1) or one crossed table of two fields, never `line` (C97). A count may carry one sum: lacking adds nothing and is counted, empty is zero, unread leaves an incomplete subtotal (C94). Summing raw line projections, combining views, sorting counts, and standalone sums are errors. *Why:* counts and totals (S170, S189) without an aggregation language (S191) or an implicit largest occurrence. *Details:* `acquisition-search/src/counts.rs`, `answer.rs`, `bind.rs`. *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/counts.rs`. 2026-09-17; E2, E4–E5 accepted 2026-09-22 (`search/LEDGER.md`): "ok, agreed"; "For sum, we don't need a grand total."
- **C105 — A count accounts for every matching item.** Known absence is `none`; a key not established is `undecided` (C93), neither omitted nor merged with a value. Every match is in at least one real bucket, `none` or `undecided`; a one-value key's buckets sum exactly to the total. *Why:* inventing a value or dropping an item breaks C93 or the total. *Details:* `acquisition-search/src/counts.rs` (selectors and vocabulary presence, E1 and E3 accepted 2026-09-22; `search/LEDGER.md`, "Holes ruled"). *Pinned:* `acquisition-search/tests/counts.rs`. Owner 2026-09-18: "Undecided in aggregate doesn't have a reason"; "a separate bucket for none"; "I agree with the two invariants".
- **C96 — Place is terms, but the realm is the search's scope, never a term, and a query never spans realms by default.** League, tab, character and container are terms like any other; which realm a search runs over is stated outside the conditions, printed back in every answer, and never hoisted from a branch of the tree. Over a store holding more than one realm, a query naming none is an error that lists them (S199), and an explicit all-realms scope exists. Within a realm the default is every league, live items. The scope block says what was searched: items, realms, leagues, locations never fetched, the oldest fetch. *Why:* a template and a total mean different things in two games (C58). The default is new here and labelled so. *Evidence:* S199. Owner 2026-09-17: "accept".
- **C97 — The vocabulary is a read of this corpus, and it is the same call as search.** Counting by `line` under the query in hand lists the templates the matching items carry, by kind — and by realm under an all-realms scope — ranked by how many items carry each, and the range per slot; several texts may be asked in one call, and every row carries the term that selects it in its realm, ready to paste, source and flag both selectable. The closed lists — fields, kinds, flags, totals with their definitions — are printed in the help. The owner's autocomplete and the agent's schema discovery are this one read. *Why:* S174 and S190 state one need; scoped discovery and the ready fragment were each the other model's reviewer's strongest praise. *Evidence:* S174, S181, S190. 2026-09-17.

## The corpus and the answer

- **C98 — No search service: a consumer holds the corpus; every answer is derived from and labelled with one consistent basis.** The basis names the store and account, one snapshot of facts with locations, the intent revision, the build, and the reference and derivation versions; a held corpus is reused only while every component it carries matches the basis of the answer in hand, checked before every answer. A write after the basis is chosen does not unmake a correctly labelled answer. Nothing is persisted first: a persisted projection is a candidate, never a preselected successor, and waits for a measured failure. *Why:* a check is structure where an event is discipline (P5); a stale result taken for current truth is C48's failure. *Evidence:* S135, S150–S153. 2026-09-17.
- **C100 — Only one answer shape: the canonical query, basis, scope, terms block, total, a view, and how to continue.** A row is compact by default — id, name, base, rarity, place by name and id, and the lines the query touched with their values (the decision view, C53) — and the caller may name fields (S193); the body only through `show` (`--against`: the why-not). Every printed id is accepted back (S198). A continuation names its basis (C98) and the query, view and order it continues, and is refused across a change. `priced` is the effective price the pricing area defines (C81), joined read-only. *Why:* an answer that carries its evidence needs no second call and no body. *Evidence:* S176, S192, S193, S196, S197, S198. *Details:* `price.rs`. 2026-09-17.
- **C101 — A missing reach is a field to add, never a general door.** There is no raw-path accessor over the body and no general collection syntax. Socket colours are asked over the whole item and within one link group (S59; `sockets.rs`). A pseudo that is not a sum or count over lines is a derived field, a named function over properties, lines and reference data (C68), listed with its definition, never a total row (C94). The site's item-reading filters not yet mapped stay an open catalogue (S42), listed as gaps. *Why:* reach is a property of the derivation (stance 6) and a derived field is the cheap class of change (S135). *Evidence:* S16, S22, S42, S58, S59. Owner 2026-09-17: "yes, accepted". *Details:* `pseudo.rs`, `defences.rs`, `sockets.rs`. *Pinned:* `tests/percentile.rs`.
- **C102 — The digest's limits register is inherited, with one stated exception, and each limit is an output.** S12, S14, S52, S53, S67, S107, S111, S177, S178: what the search says on meeting each is the register's wording; the exception is S111 under a shipped total, where a moved template looks like a lacking one (C94). Binding occurrences does not say which twin is local (S52, S72); a site defence bound is inexact (C99); a pseudo needing the mod behind a line is out of reach (S52); judgment — what is good, worth keeping or legacy — is the user's (stance 4, C106). The search never fuzzy-matches, values, judges legacy, fetches, acts on the stash, or recovers the mod behind a line. *Why:* a limit stated at the answer is read; one in a document is not. 2026-09-17.
- **C106 — Reference data admits game data and community convention, never judgment.** Data is what the game files state (base ranges); convention is a concept with a published community definition (equipment, the trade site's categories). Both enter under C68 from a registered surface (C79), through the admission test the crate's doc states, and remove nothing: the user composes their own meaning from terms. Judgment — what is good, what it is worth, what to keep — is never embedded. Legacy stays the user's (S178, C107). *Why:* C102 gave base defences to the user while C101 built a field on them. *Details:* `lib.rs`, `class.rs`, `category.rs`. *Pinned:* `tests/class.rs`, `tests/category.rs`. Owner 2026-09-18: "I approve the proposed three-way line as amended."
- **C107 — Legacy is the user's word; a source's own label may be a field.** No field, term or output of the search says "legacy" (S178, R8). What a registered source prints about an item — the variant label Path of Building gives a unique (`Pre 3.23.0`, `Current`) — is convention under C106 and may back a derived field (C101) that prints the label and the source's version; an item that fits an old and a current variant, or none, is undecided with its reason (C93). *Why:* the measurement found a source for a unique's variants and none, for any kind of item, that says what the game can still produce. *Evidence:* `search/legacy/README.md` at `77bca830`. Owner 2026-09-18: "legacy is not well-defined enough to become a field. Variant is better."
- **C117 — GGG's facts about a tab are fields, and the tool says nothing of what the site lists.** `tab.public` (the listing's `metadata.public`; a substash's is its tab's; an item on a character lacks it), `tab.remove_only` (the suffix GGG puts on the name, N47) and `price.side` join the index; whether the site would list an item stays a park, the inference the agent's, labelled. *Why:* both second-round Opus runs made "would a buyer see it" their worst fault and inferred it from `has:priced` and `price show` at four calls a task, while the store held the flag. Owner, 2026-10-18, on the six as recommended: "I accept all 6. Go ahead." *Details:* `brainstorming-notes/38-the-answer-an-agent-reads.md`, step 4.
- **C118 — An answer says how old a row is and what kind of place it could not see.** A row carries its location's fetch age beside `place` (text in the CLI, an epoch in JSON, C53); the scope line says the never fetched by tab type, the largest named and the rest counted, and points at the list that exists. Why a location was never fetched — by policy or not yet — is left out until a seat asks. *Why:* a map question got the same flat line as a ring question over a map stash 61 of 2,550 fetched; "where do I pick it up" needs the age. Owner, 2026-10-18, on the six as recommended: "I accept all 6. Go ahead." *Details:* `brainstorming-notes/38-the-answer-an-agent-reads.md`, step 2.
